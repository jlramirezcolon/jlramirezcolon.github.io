From 2020 to 2021, I was Team Leader of the Science and Research Branch for the University of Puerto Rico, Río Piedras, RockSat-X student payload.

## The mission

Our payload set out to collect microparticle samples at the Kármán line, the edge of space, for post-flight analysis in astrogenomics research. We also aimed to bring the Oxford Nanopore VolTRAX, an automated device that prepares DNA for sequencing, to technology readiness level 9, meaning flight-proven, by amplifying and analyzing that genetic material. Our success criteria were video confirmation of the payload's deployment and retraction, and the VolTRAX surviving the flight.

## What I did

I led the team's scientific activities and represented the science team in mission progress meetings. I developed and delivered technical flight-readiness presentations, and contributed to the mission's success criteria, contamination mitigation, and scientific planning.

At the bench, I performed an end-to-end Lambda DNA control sequencing workflow on an Oxford Nanopore MinION Mk1B, and supported experimental preparation with VolTRAX.

## Launch and recovery

The payload launched on August 19, 2021, from NASA Wallops Flight Facility aboard a Terrier-Improved Malemute sounding rocket. After the flight it was recovered from the ocean, and the team began evaluating how spaceflight, extreme acceleration, and atmospheric re-entry had affected its mechanical and electrical systems.

The success of the 2021 recovery paved the way for later UPR aerospace research toward fully autonomous, high-altitude sequencing systems, using tools like the VolTRAX for independent DNA analysis in space environments.
