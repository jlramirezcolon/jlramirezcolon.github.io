## Background

What if a fruit native to the Caribbean could help solve a water contamination crisis? *Crescentia cujete*, the calabash tree, has deep roots in Indigenous Caribbean culture.

## The problem

Puerto Rico has historically had some of the worst drinking-water quality and Safe Drinking Water Act violation rates in the United States. A landmark 2015 study by the [Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC)](https://www.nrdc.org/resources/threats-tap-drinking-water-violations-puerto-rico) found that nearly 99.5% of residents relied on water systems with Safe Drinking Water Act violations, and nearly 69.4% received water that violated federal health-based standards. Tap water and local waterways have faced elevated levels of volatile organic compounds, coliform bacteria, disinfection byproducts, heavy metals such as lead and copper, and industrial pollutants.

Communities across the Caribbean and Latin America need affordable access to clean water, and conventional treatment methods for removing metals like copper can be expensive.

## What I did

As a high school intern in the Ramos Laboratory at the University of Puerto Rico, Humacao, I made activated carbon from the shells of the calabash fruit and tested it as a filter for copper-contaminated water. I designed a 3D-printed filter to hold the material and measured how much copper it removed using atomic absorption spectroscopy.

## What I found

The calabash carbon removed up to 83% of the copper from water samples, even outperforming a commercial brand of activated carbon, at a fraction of the cost of conventional treatment. That points to real potential for communities that need affordable access to clean water.

## Recognition

The project represented Puerto Rico at two international science fairs. It earned the 4th Grand Award in Environmental Engineering at the Intel International Science and Engineering Fair 2017 in Los Angeles, and the Second Prize for International Participants in Environmental Science and Engineering at the 32nd China Adolescents Science and Technology Innovation Contest in Hangzhou.
